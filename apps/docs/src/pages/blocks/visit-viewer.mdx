One clinical visit is often split across several DHIS2 program stages, such as
a child health visit recorded as a vaccination stage and a growth-monitoring
stage. Visit Viewer lays out the visit as your app understands it, not stage by
stage.

The visits in these examples are fictional. The Schedule visit and Open
buttons are illustrative only.

Visit Viewer is UI only. It doesn't fetch, read program or stage metadata,
filter, sort or group. Your app builds the visit array however it likes, then
branches on loading, error and empty states and maps visits into the parts in
JSX. It needs no app-runtime or query-client provider.

## Composition

Every part is a plain element that takes its native props and `className`,
and every slot is a child: there are no component props, `visits` arrays or
render callbacks.

```tsx
import {
    VisitDate,
    VisitItem,
    VisitItemActions,
    VisitItemBody,
    VisitItemContent,
    VisitItemHeader,
    VisitItemTitle,
    VisitItemTrigger,
    VisitList,
    VisitStatus,
    VisitViewer,
    VisitViewerEmpty,
    VisitViewerHeader,
    VisitViewerTitle,
} from '@/components/visit-viewer'

export function Visits({ visits }: { visits: MyVisit[] }) {
    return (
        <VisitViewer calendar={systemCalendar}>
            <VisitViewerHeader>
                <VisitViewerTitle>Visits</VisitViewerTitle>
            </VisitViewerHeader>
            {visits.length === 0 ? (
                <VisitViewerEmpty>No visits recorded yet.</VisitViewerEmpty>
            ) : (
                <VisitList>
                    {visits.map((visit) => (
                        <VisitItem key={visit.id} status={visit.status}>
                            <VisitItemHeader>
                                <VisitItemTitle>{visit.name}</VisitItemTitle>
                                <VisitDate value={visit.date} />
                                <VisitStatus variant={visit.status} />
                                <VisitItemActions>
                                    <VisitItemTrigger />
                                </VisitItemActions>
                            </VisitItemHeader>
                            <VisitItemBody>
                                {/* services, meta */}
                            </VisitItemBody>
                            <VisitItemContent>
                                {/* facts, notes */}
                            </VisitItemContent>
                        </VisitItem>
                    ))}
                </VisitList>
            )}
        </VisitViewer>
    )
}
```

- **Chrome.** `VisitViewer` is a `<section>`, named by `VisitViewerTitle` when
  you add one. `VisitViewerHeader` puts `VisitViewerTitle` and
  `VisitViewerDescription` at the start and `VisitViewerActions` at the end.
  `VisitViewerToolbar` holds your own filters, search or view toggles.
  `VisitViewerGroup` and `VisitViewerGroupLabel` are a section and heading for
  the groups you made, such as Overdue, Upcoming and Past. `VisitViewerEmpty`
  holds your empty-state copy and actions.
- **Cards.** `VisitList` is an `<ol>` and `VisitItem` an `<li>` card. Pass
  `status` to set `data-status` (a skipped visit is muted). `VisitItemHeader`,
  `VisitItemTitle`, `VisitItemBody` and `VisitItemActions` are the card's
  regions.
- **Headings.** `VisitViewerTitle`, `VisitViewerGroupLabel` and
  `VisitItemTitle` are level 2, 3 and 4 headings; pass `level` to fit your
  page's outline.

### Opening a visit's details

`VisitItemTrigger` opens and closes `VisitItemContent` by mouse or keyboard,
and sets `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`. Its text is your children, or
"Details". Each `VisitItem` is uncontrolled by default; pass `defaultOpen` to
start open, or `open` with `onOpenChange` to control it from your app.

## Visit parts

These parts work inside a `VisitItem`, in a table cell, or on their own.

- **`VisitDate`** takes the date or date-time exactly as stored
  (`2026-11-12` or `2026-11-12T14:30:00.000`) and renders a `<time>`. It reads
  the day from the string, never through `Date`, so the day never shifts with
  the browser's time zone. It formats in the `calendar` and `locale` set on
  `VisitViewer` (Gregorian outside one); pass `calendar` or `locale` to a single
  date to override them, `showTime` to add the stored time and `prefix` for
  words such as "Due". Read the system calendar yourself and pass it to
  `VisitViewer`; `useVisitViewer()` returns the settings in effect.
- **`VisitStatus`** is a badge for `completed`, `in-progress`, `scheduled`,
  `overdue` or `skipped`. Each pairs an icon with words, never colour alone:
  your children, or the status's localized name.
- **`VisitServices`** and **`VisitService`** list what was done in the visit,
  such as Vaccination and Growth monitoring. Pass `status` to a service when a
  visit was partly done; it shows an icon and the status in words.
- **`VisitMeta`** is a muted line such as "Ngelehun CHC · Nurse Amina · 14:30".
  It puts a dot between its children and hides when they render nothing.
- **`VisitFacts`**, **`VisitFact`**, **`VisitFactLabel`** and
  **`VisitFactValue`** show values picked from across the visit's stages as a
  `<dl>`. `VisitFactValue` takes a `state`: `value` (the default, isolated in a
  `<bdi>`), `missing` ("Not recorded"), `not-performed` (a dash, read as "Not
  performed") or `restricted` ("Unavailable"). Children replace those words.
- **`VisitNotes`**, **`VisitNote`**, **`VisitNoteMeta`** and
  **`VisitNoteText`** list the notes written across the visit.

## Table

Visit Viewer has no table of its own. Compose the
[Table](/blocks/table) primitives from `@/components/dhis2/ui/table` and put
`VisitDate`, `VisitStatus`, `VisitServices`, `VisitFactValue` and
`VisitItemActions` in your cells. Outside a `VisitFact`, `VisitFactValue`
renders a `<span>`, and so does `VisitService` outside `VisitServices`, so no
cell holds a stray `<dd>` or `<li>`.
